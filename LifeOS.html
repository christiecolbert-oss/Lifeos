ADHD & Habit Guidance ✦
🧠
How ADHD Affects Habits
ADHD brains struggle with time blindness, task initiation, and working memory — not willpower. Your brain craves novelty and immediate rewards. The key is building systems that work with your brain, not against it.
⚡
The 2-Minute Rule
If a habit takes less than 2 minutes, do it immediately. Can't start? Shrink the habit to its smallest form. "Exercise" becomes "put on shoes." Starting is the hardest part — momentum does the rest.
🔗
Habit Stacking
Attach new habits to existing ones. "After I pour my morning coffee, I will write 3 intentions." This uses your existing routine as an anchor so you don't have to rely on remembering.
⏰
Time Blocking for ADHD
Try 25 min focus → 5 min break (Pomodoro). Schedule your hardest tasks during peak energy hours. Most ADHD people peak mid-morning or late evening.
🌅
The Anchor Morning Routine
Keep it short (15–30 min). Suggested flow: Wake → Water → Move (5 min) → 3 priorities → Begin. Same order every day builds automatic behavior.
🎯
One Thing At a Time
Each day, pick ONE non-negotiable task. Everything else is a bonus. This prevents the "all-or-nothing" spiral where missing one thing ruins the whole day.
🏆
Reward Yourself Immediately
ADHD brains need immediate rewards. After completing a task, give yourself something enjoyable right away. Make the reward feel proportional and real.
📵
Environment Design
Remove friction for good habits, add friction for distractions. Your environment should do the reminding — not your brain.
🔄
Never Miss Twice
Missing once is an accident. Missing twice starts a new bad habit. When you miss, your only job is to show up the next day in the smallest possible way.
📋
